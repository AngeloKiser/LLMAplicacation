🔍 Question 1: Explain in detail the Transformer architecture and how the self-attention mechanism replaced RNNs and CNNs in NLP tasks.
🧠 Answer: A transformer is a deep learning model that uses attention mechanisms to process sequential data. The original transformer was introduced by Ashish Vaswani et al. (2017) for machine translation tasks, but has since been applied to a wide range of natural language processing (NLP) tasks such as question answering, text classification, and sentence generation.

The transformer consists of an encoder and a decoder. The encoder takes as input a sequence of words and outputs a sequence of hidden states, each representing a different word in the input sequence. The decoder takes as input the output of the encoder and the target sequence (e.g., a sentence or phrase), and produces an output sequence that represents the final prediction.

One of the key components of the transformer is the self-attention mechanism. This mechanism allows the model to attend to different parts of the input sequence at different times, based on their relevance to the current output. For example, if the input sequence is “I went to the store and bought some apples,” the model might attend to the words “went” and “store” when predicting the word “to” in the output sequence.

The transformer architecture has several advantages over previous NLP models, such as recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and convolutional neural networks (CNNs). One advantage is its ability to handle long sequences more effectively than RNNs, which can suffer from vanishing gradients and other issues when dealing with long input sequences. Another advantage is its ability to capture long-range dependencies between words in a sequence, which is important for many NLP tasks such as machine translation and question answering.

Overall, the transformer architecture and self-attention mechanism have revolutionized the field of NLP, allowing for state-of-the-art performance on a wide range of tasks.
